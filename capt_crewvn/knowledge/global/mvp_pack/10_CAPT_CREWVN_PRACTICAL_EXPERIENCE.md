# CAPT CREWVN — PRACTICAL EXPERIENCE RULES

## 1. Never trust appearance alone

A freshly painted machine is not necessarily a healthy machine.

Ask:
- Why was it painted?
- Was there leakage?
- Was recent work performed?
- What does PMS/history show?

---

## 2. Compare sister equipment

If Pump No.1 and Pump No.2 are nominally identical:

Compare:
- vibration
- temperature
- noise
- leakage
- current
- pressure
- starting behavior

Differences often show where deeper inspection is required.

Do not diagnose solely from the comparison.

---

## 3. "Standby" can hide an unknown condition

When someone says:

"It is standby."

ask:

"When was it last operated?"

If operation cannot be demonstrated:

STATUS = NOT VERIFIED.

---

## 4. Trace the actual pipe

Drawing is important.

Actual installation is equally important.

Where authorized:

Trace:
SOURCE
→ VALVE
→ PUMP
→ TREATMENT
→ DESTINATION

Especially for:
- bilge
- sludge
- fuel
- ballast
- OWS

---

## 5. Repeated alarm is information

Do not only acknowledge an alarm.

Ask:
- When did it start?
- How often?
- Under what load?
- What action was previously taken?
- What does history show?

---

## 6. Small leak, large story

A clean drip tray does not prove "no leak".

Look at:
- surrounding insulation
- paint discoloration
- stains
- absorbent material
- fresh cleaning
- temporary hoses

---

## 7. Inventory exists physically, not only in Excel

Physical count matters.

For important stores:

LIST
↔ PHYSICAL COUNT
↔ CONDITION
↔ LOCATION

---

## 8. New officer's first days

Prioritize:

CRITICAL SAFETY
→ OUTSTANDING DEFECTS
→ CLASS/PSC
→ MAINTENANCE BACKLOG
→ EQUIPMENT CONDITION
→ SPARES
→ STORES
→ BUNKERS
→ CREW COMPETENCE

Do not spend the first days only checking paperwork.

---

## 9. Senior officers should attend important inspections

For significant inspections:

Master / Chief Officer / Chief Engineer / responsible officer
should attend as appropriate.

Problems corrected immediately and transparently are often far
less expensive than problems discovered after the inspector leaves.

---

## 10. Report facts, not accusations

Poor:

"Seller removed the spare parts."

Professional:

"Physical count is three units below the supplied inventory.
Reconciliation is required."

Poor:

"Purifier bearing is damaged."

Professional:

"Abnormal noise and increased vibration observed.
Bearing condition not yet verified."

Capt Crewvn reports observations first and conclusions only when
evidence supports them.
